# Expansion from five benchmarks to the remaining 390 lessons

Do not auto-generate the remaining guides. Approve the instructional standard and five benchmark experiences first, using a South African Grades 8–12 educator, a commerce/financial subject reviewer and a language/accessibility reviewer. These are required human review functions, not claims that this agent's self-review replaces them.

## Gate 1 · benchmark moderation and classroom pilots

Read each original source alongside its private guide. Verify explanations, maths, age appropriateness, privacy, prerequisite diagnostics and the exact selected authored task. Resolve the prioritised improvement register through separately reviewed source proposals where needed. Pilot one period with a typical large class and low-technology materials; pilot both periods of the numerical/decision guides. Record actual minutes, learner misconceptions, samples of reasoning with consent/redaction, preparation effort and quiet-participation access. Revise and revalidate; only an authorised educational owner changes the review status to approved. No automatic production publication follows approval.

## Gate 2 · calibrate a small sequence

Choose two adjacent lessons per grade, ten guides total, so continuity and prerequisite assumptions can be checked against actual learner experience. At least one pair must include a financial/legal reference and one a heavy practical workload. Two designers independently plan one shared lesson, compare the result with the standard, and agree a moderation rubric. Re-estimate workload and classroom duration from that exercise before committing programme dates.

## Gate 3 · phased development

Develop by coherent term sequences, preserving Grade 9's actual 75 lessons. Prioritise missing objectives, mathematical ambiguity, current-reference risks and high-contact-time lessons. Each guide needs full source reading, named objective-to-task mapping, prerequisite diagnosis, lesson-specific explanation/discussion, realistic activity scope, formative evidence and links to adjacent learning. Source changes remain separate PRs with curriculum-owner approval; no renumbering or response-key migration is implicit.

An initial capacity model is 4–6 hours of source reading and design, 1–2 hours subject/educational moderation and 0.5–1 hour accessibility/engineering QA per ordinary guide. Complex two-period or legally sensitive lessons may take 8–12+ hours. At 390 guides, the ordinary estimate is 2,145–3,510 person-hours before pilots and revisions. A team providing 80 productive hours per week would need roughly 27–44 weeks plus pilot/rework time; two people providing 40 combined hours would need 54–88 weeks. These are planning ranges, not guaranteed delivery dates. Re-estimate after the ten-guide calibration and include school-calendar constraints.

Release review batches of about ten guides, with no obligation to publish a batch merely because it exists. Every batch includes peer review and sampled classroom trials; rotate grades and classroom conditions. A named educational lead owns scope and accuracy; authors cannot be the sole approvers of their own guides. Keep a coverage ledger against all 395 stable lesson IDs and record who reviewed what version.

## Gate 4 · protected release and verification

Version guides separately from source books. Validate exact catalogue linkage, source fingerprints/excerpts, complete teaching fields, 45-minute periods, unchanged response identities and review status. Encrypt private content for this public repository, retain its key securely outside Git and provision the private server file only through an authorised non-production release process first. Test fail-closed access, a real assigned educator, an unassigned learner, role revocation and institution boundaries using isolated role fixtures. Synthetic UI tests do not replace that gate.

For each batch run runtime integrity, Node tests, typecheck, lint, build and relevant Playwright cases. Verify mobile/keyboard/print on all new layout patterns, and sample plain-language and screen-reader use with people. Compare public client bundles against private text sentinels. Do not turn captured learner responses into correctness or change assessment rubrics through a guide release.

## Completion criteria and ongoing maintenance

All 395 guides eventually need named educational approval, source links, validated timing, adaptations and verified classroom evidence; inventory coverage alone is insufficient. Maintain a dated official-reference ledger for financial/legal facts and recheck when rates or rules change. Keep learner independence, the five-year progression and paper-based classroom delivery in the review rubric. Track new source versions by fingerprint and explicitly invalidate/review affected guides rather than silently attaching old guidance to revised lessons. Production promotion remains a separate authorised action.
