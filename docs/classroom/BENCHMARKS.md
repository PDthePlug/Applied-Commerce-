# Benchmark selection and educational review

The full block content, authored objectives, vocabulary, narratives, tasks and checkpoints were inspected before choosing these five lessons. Exact source references and excerpt checksums are in `benchmark-manifest.v1.json`; the private guides retain the cited excerpts. All are `draft-review`, with ten quality self-review entries each. Human educator approval is pending.

The table uses short descriptive titles for scanning; the application and inventory display each exact original title. Selection was based on the source activities above, not the title alone.

Each developed guide contains learner objectives, diagnostic prerequisites, a central explanation, educator preparation, source vocabulary, misconceptions, a South African context, privacy boundaries, timed teacher and learner actions, observable participation, worthwhile discussion with follow-up, practical scope/output, reflection, secure/partial/not-yet-evidenced reasoning, a next teaching move, six forms of adaptation, previous/next lesson continuity and independent learning. A print view and a learner-facing presentation mode use the same versioned guide.

The guides deliberately differ. Grade 8 uses contextual classification and reasons; Grade 9 examines a values trade-off; Grade 10 models and compares numerical growth; Grade 11 designs a mechanism and tests its risk; Grade 12 evaluates a weighted model's limits. They do not repeat a generic group-discussion template with changed titles. Two-period sequences provide a coherent first-period outcome and time to revisit reasoning.

The ten acceptance questions are documented inside every private guide with evidence and a disposition: reasonable teacher preparation; accessible language; active participation; clear central concept; task alignment; worthwhile questions; plausible time; observable understanding; progression; independent learning. These are agent self-review judgements. Time estimates, ordinary-teacher usability and learner language need an independent educator and classroom pilot before approval. No approved badge or mastery mark is asserted.

The existing authored exercises remain intact. New paper/board scaffolds are labelled added support and do not create response IDs or overwrite learner work. Personal reflection can use fictional or redacted information. Indicative reasoning is formative guidance, not an automatic marking key.
