# Focused official-source and calculation checks

No learner-source correction is applied. These checks distinguish dated general guidance, specific published product terms and hypothetical arithmetic.

The NCR's [January 2022 borrowing guidance](https://www.ncr.org.za/index.php/latest-news/8-latest-news/125-consumers-are-encouraged-to-borrow-wisely-and-start-saving) advises considering repayment affordability, obtaining the total costs/terms and not taking additional credit just because offered. Its historical rate comment is not used as current information. Its [June 2022 credit-record article](https://www.ncr.org.za/index.php/latest-news/8-latest-news/128-keep-your-credit-bureau-record-clean-regardless-of-the-rising-cost-of-living) distinguishes credit-bureau information from the lender's decision and discusses checking/correcting records. These support the general distinctions; procedural deadlines, quotas and all current legal conditions are not exhaustively verified here.

The official [fixed-rate RSA savings-bond terms in the published 2025 file](https://www.rsaretailbonds.gov.za/assets/docs/2025_Terms_and_Conditions_Fixed_Rate_Retail_Savings_Bond.pdf) were scanned, so OCR was followed by visual comparison of pages 9 and 12. Clause 5 states a R1,000 minimum for that series. Clause 13 makes early withdrawal conditional: normally after twelve months with a penalty, with the stated exceptional first-year route. This supports a specific fixed-rate example and the importance of access conditions, not a universal rule for all bond series or every investment product. The [official FAQ](https://www.rsaretailbonds.gov.za/faq.aspx) contains both generic and product-specific statements, so the actual series terms must be identified. No current interest rate is certified.

For the fee exercise, one explicit educational approximation subtracts the stated annual fees from the assumed 8%, giving 7.7% and 5.5%. Treating these as nominal annual rates divided monthly, with R500 at each month end for 360 months, gives about R701,326.14 and R456,805.95, difference R244,520.19. Contributions total R180,000. This omits tax, inflation, other charges and changing returns/fees; it is not exact daily TER accounting or a forecast. Other consistently specified conventions give different totals. The annual R25,000 examples yield R625 and R75 at the stated expense percentages.

For reinvestment, R70,000/R90,000×100≈77.78%, rounded to 78%. Simple payback is about 1.29 years only under a continuing incremental-profit assumption. This is not a complete annualised return calculation for staggered cash flows or a guarantee of R70,000 forever. The first three table rows show R145,000 profit, R90,000 reinvested and R55,000 taken home; all four rows reinvest R120,000, so the period needs to be explicit.
